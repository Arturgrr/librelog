## Dictation

- **Clean up with AI**, in **Settings → Dictation**, removes filler words, fixes punctuation and applies the corrections you say, such as "no, I mean Friday", with the AI model you chose for summaries and chat. It works with local models. If the model is slow or fails, your words are typed as you said them.
- **Edit selected text by voice**: select some text, then dictate an instruction such as "make this shorter" and the selection is rewritten. If the edit fails, the selection is left as it was. On macOS and Linux.
- **Use what's on screen** reads the window you dictate into, so AI cleanup and edits spell its names and terms correctly. Password fields, password managers, private windows and the apps and websites you list are never read, and nothing read is kept.
- **Replacements**, in **Settings → Dictionary**: when dictation hears a phrase, it writes your text instead, like "my email" for your address. They apply with or without AI cleanup.
- Dictation now sends your Dictionary words to the transcription model for the whole recording, not only the live preview.
- **Learn from your corrections** gives the words you dictate most a better chance of being heard right, and when you fix a dictated word in the field, suggests it for your Dictionary. What it learns stays on your computer.
- Dictation fits its text to what is already in the field, adding a space or a capital letter where one is needed. To do that it reads the text before the cursor, never in password fields, private windows or the apps and websites you list in **Settings → Dictation**.
- With **Edit selected text by voice** on but no AI model chosen, dictating over a selection types your words over it.
- A dictation that could not be transcribed is no longer lost. It stays in the dictation history with its audio for 24 hours, with a **Retry** button.
- Every feature above is off until you turn it on.

## Annotate

- When you stop an annotation, its image and its transcript are prepared right away, so **Option + Shift + A** pastes them at once. Your local transcription model now starts loading as soon as an annotation starts recording and stays loaded until the annotation is ready. Before, a model released after a minute of rest had to load again after you stopped. Dictation keeps its model loaded the same way while you speak.
- macOS no longer slows Librelog down in the background while an annotation records or is being prepared.
- **Option + Shift + A** could paste the previous annotation when the app had lost track of the one being prepared. It now waits for the new one.

## Notes and folders

- Edits typed just before quitting are saved: quitting from the tray or the Dock, pressing ⌘Q twice or ⌘⇧Q, and **File → Quit** or Control + Q on Linux all save the note first.
- Renaming, moving or deleting a folder could also reach folders with a similar name, such as `Q1_2026` and `Q1-2026`, or `Work` and `work`. It now changes only that folder.

## Linux

- Dictation types straight into text fields through accessibility, as it was meant to. A bug made every dictation go through the clipboard instead. Apps built with Qt, such as KDE apps, still get dictation through the clipboard, so accented letters are not garbled.
- When a field accepts only part of a dictation, Librelog tells you instead of pasting it again.

## Windows

- Dictation checks that the same field still has the focus right before typing, and tells you whether Windows blocked all of it or only part.

## Known limitations

- These changes were written without a Mac, a Linux desktop or a Windows computer to try them on. Automated checks build and test them, but nobody has dictated or annotated with them yet.
- Editing selected text by voice and reading the screen are not available on Windows. Browsers are never read.
- Logging out or shutting down the Mac while Librelog is open can still end it before the last edits are saved.
- Librelog is not notarized by Apple yet. The first launch needs **Open Anyway** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.
- On a Mac, Librelog needs Apple silicon (M1 or later).

Parts of this release come from [Anarlog](https://github.com/fastrepl/anarlog), the MIT-licensed project Librelog is based on.
