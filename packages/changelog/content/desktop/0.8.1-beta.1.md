## People in the same room are told apart

- A meeting recorded in person, with everyone sharing one microphone, used to come out as a single speaker. Now, when you stop the recording, Librelog separates the voices and labels them **Speaker 1**, **Speaker 2** and so on. Name or merge them with **Assign speakers…**.
- This works with every transcription model, including the live ones, and needs no participant list. It runs on your computer after the meeting, when **Settings → Meetings → Separate speakers after meetings** is on and the recording is kept.
- When you record alone, the transcript stays as it was: you are still **You**, and no second speaker is invented.
- For an older note with one recording, right-click **Transcript** and choose **Separate speakers** to split the voices of the transcript you already have, without transcribing it again. **Re-transcribe** separates them too.
- A listed participant count is now an upper limit for the people in the room, never an exact number.

## Linux (beta)

<banner title="Not tried on a real desktop yet" variant="warning">
The Linux app was written and built without a Linux desktop to run it on. Automated checks build the packages and install them on Fedora, but nobody has recorded a meeting, dictated, taken a screenshot or annotated with it yet. Expect rough edges, and tell us what breaks, with your distribution and desktop.
</banner>

- Every release now has three packages for x86_64 computers: an **RPM** for Fedora, a **deb** for Ubuntu 24.04 or later and Debian, and an **AppImage** for other distributions. [Install on Linux](https://github.com/Arturgrr/librelog/blob/main/docs/install-linux.mdx) has the steps.
- Meetings, transcription with local models, speaker separation, summaries, dictation, history and Insights, automations, the CLI and the MCP server are all there. Local models use your GPU through Vulkan when it works, and the CPU otherwise.
- **Built-in models** for summaries and chat run on Linux, with the model server shipped inside the packages.
- **Dictation** types into the focused field through accessibility. Where a field does not take text that way, such as terminals, browsers and Electron apps, Librelog pastes through the clipboard and puts back what was there. On Wayland, your desktop asks once to let Librelog press the paste keys.
- **Shortcuts** on Wayland go through your desktop: it asks you to approve them, once for all of them, and may let you choose other keys. Settings shows the keys your desktop chose when they differ. To cancel a dictation there, press Control + Alt + Period.
- **Screenshots and recordings** use your desktop's screenshot tool and screen-sharing dialog. Recordings are MP4, or WebM when no H.264 encoder is installed, and GIFs work. Recordings have no sound, countdown or outline yet.
- **Annotate** records the screen you share. The capsule and your red marks are part of the recording, and the key frames are still written with clean marks. Holding the shortcut draws for up to 8 seconds at a time.
- The AppImage updates itself. The RPM and deb show that a new version is available and open its download page; install it the same way as the first one.
- On GNOME without the AppIndicator extension there is no tray icon, so closing the window minimizes Librelog instead of hiding it. Quit with **File → Quit Librelog** or Control + Q.

## Known limitations

- On Wayland, Librelog's windows run through XWayland so its floating windows stay in place. With fractional scaling they can look blurry. Start Librelog with `LIBRELOG_WAYLAND=1` to run natively; Annotate then has no drawing overlay, and the dictation indicator and the meeting bar go where your desktop puts them.
- Recording the screen as MP4 on Fedora needs an H.264 encoder, such as `gstreamer1-plugin-openh264`. Without one, recordings are WebM.
- The RPM is not signed. Check it against the release's `SHA256SUMS.txt`.
- Not on Linux: App Lock, Apple's transcription models, Apple Reminders and Contacts, the notch indicator, and replacing the system's screenshot shortcuts. There are no packages for ARM64.
- Speaker separation for a room happens after the meeting, not while it is recorded. Someone who speaks for less than about 12 seconds in total is merged into the main voice. In a room, your own voice is one of the numbered speakers until you name it.
- Speaker separation was tested with recorded samples, not in a real room and not on a Mac.
- Librelog is not notarized by Apple yet. The first launch on a Mac needs **Open Anyway** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.
- On a Mac, Librelog needs Apple silicon (M1 or later).
